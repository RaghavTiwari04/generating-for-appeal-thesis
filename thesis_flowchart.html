Greeting cards, generated to actually sell.

A generative pipeline that first learns what makes a card commercially appealing from real marketplace listings, then generates new cards steered by that learned model, and finally tests whether the steering worked with an LLM-as-judge panel (the pre-registered human study it replaced still lives in the code). Overview

The whole argument, in one line

Thesis

If a model can predict which greeting cards people would actually buy, then wiring that model into a generator as a best-of-N reranker should produce cards rated as more purchase-worthy than naïve AI — approaching human bestsellers. Every node here is a decision in service of testing that claim cleanly.

How to read this

Three phases, top to bottom. Phase 1 builds the appeal model; Phase 2 spends it at generation time; Phase 3 measures whether it mattered. The coral loop marks where generation feeds back into the learned model.

Reproducibility: seeded RNG · pinned model revisions · W&B run logs · versioned prompts.   Definition of done: predictor ρ≥0.4 vs purchase intent · 4-condition eval · ≥3 ablations · <60s demo.
